# Operators

Each operator is a lever you pull on the frame. Pull it against a specific sacred assumption or soft constraint, not against the document in general. If an operator produces nothing useful for this document, write one honest idea and say it is weak; do not skip the operator.

Quota per operator: `sane` 1, `bold` 2, `unhinged` 3.

## 1. Invert (code `I`)

Flip a direction the document takes for granted: who does what to whom, order, cause and effect, input and output, viewer and author, buyer and seller.

Prompts:
- What if the audience made it and the author watched?
- What if we started at the end?
- What if the thing we hide is the thing we show?

## 2. 10x / 100x (code `X`)

Multiply one number in the document by 10 or 100: count, speed, audience, price, length, frequency. Then also divide one by 10. The interesting part is what breaks, because what breaks shows what the plan really depends on.

Prompts:
- 100 of these instead of 3: what would have to be automatic?
- The same result in a tenth of the time: what gets cut?
- Ten times the audience: what format would they need?

## 3. Delete the core (code `D`)

Remove the thing the document is about, or its most expensive part, and see what is left. Often the remainder is a cheaper, sharper product.

Prompts:
- Remove the subject. Does the method still prove the point?
- Remove the biggest risk entirely instead of managing it.
- Remove the container (slides, pages, app, meeting).

## 4. Steal from another industry (code `S`)

Take a solved pattern from a field far away (film, sport, music, fashion, games, medicine, aviation, retail, religion, theatre) and transplant it whole. Name the field and the exact pattern you stole.

Prompts:
- How would a film studio, a sports broadcaster, a record label release this?
- Which industry already solved "show the process, not just the product"?

## 5. Make it free (code `F`)

Make the paid, scarce or guarded part free, or give the whole thing away. Then ask what new thing becomes valuable once that is free. Watch Reversibility: what is given away publicly cannot be taken back.

Prompts:
- Give away the engine, sell the judgement.
- What would someone pay for if the main output cost nothing?

## 6. Make the customer do it (code `U`)

Move a step from the maker to the user, viewer or customer, so their effort becomes the experience (IKEA, Wikipedia, open source, quizzes, voting).

Prompts:
- What could the viewer choose, guess, assemble or vote on?
- What part of our work is actually the fun part for them?

## 7. Time travel 10 years (code `T`)

Imagine the same goal in 10 years, when today's hard part is cheap. What is the obvious version then? Pull it back to now. Also run it backwards: how would this have been done 30 years ago, and does the old way have something we lost?

Prompts:
- In 10 years, what would be generated per person instead of made once?
- What artifact will matter more than the output itself?

Rule: time travel never invents facts about real people or companies (no forecasts presented as fact).

## 8. Constraint flip (code `K`)

Take a soft constraint the document fights against and make it the headline, the feature, or the game. Hard constraints are not flipped; they become the stage.

Prompts:
- Which limitation would be the most impressive thing to brag about?
- Which rule could the audience experience directly?

## Merging

When two operators produce the same move, keep one id, list the other as "merged into". When two ideas are stronger together (one enables the other), keep both and note the link; step 5 may present them as one bet.
