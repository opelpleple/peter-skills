# Assumption tags

Every assumption gets exactly one tag. When two seem to fit, pick the weaker one (habit is weaker than convention, convention is weaker than data, data is weaker than physics). It is cheaper to promote a tag in the question ladder than to discover late that a "law" was a habit.

## physics

A constraint that holds no matter who you are or which tool you use.

- Laws of nature and mathematics (a day has 24 hours, a budget is finite, a screen has a fixed number of pixels).
- Human perception and cognition, at the level of well replicated findings (attention is limited and mostly serial, people cannot read and listen to different words at the same time without loss, the end of an experience weighs heavily in memory).
- Hard limits of the medium (a printed page cannot move, a phone screen is small, a talk has a fixed length).
- Law in the legal sense, when it really applies (a regulation, a contract, a licence).

Test: would this still be true if every competitor, tool and template disappeared tomorrow? If yes, physics.

## data

True because evidence says so, here, now.

- Measured numbers, user research, past results, logs, documents.
- Must name its source (file, URL, dataset). No source, no data tag: re-tag as habit.

Test: can you point to the evidence in under a minute? Would it change if the evidence changed?

## convention

Inherited from outside: an industry norm, a template, a tool's default, "best practice".

- Slide templates, report formats, org chart shapes, standard meeting lengths, default component libraries.

Test: does the assumption exist because a tool or a crowd made it the default? Did anyone in the room choose it?

## habit

What this person, team or model does by default, usually because it worked once.

- "We always start with a kickoff", "I always write the summary last", and for AI systems: whatever the model produces when the prompt leaves a decision open (the most probable layout, word or structure).

Test: if you asked "who decided this?", would the honest answer be "nobody, it just happens"?

## Small example (generic)

Problem: "Our weekly meeting runs long."

| # | Assumption | Tag | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The meeting is 60 minutes | convention | calendar default |
| 2 | Everyone needs to attend | habit | invite list copied from last quarter |
| 3 | Status must be shared live | habit | nobody tried written status |
| 4 | Attention to passive listening declines over time | physics | attention limits; the exact minute count is contested, so no fixed number is claimed |
| 5 | Decisions take most of the time | data | last 4 meeting notes, 70% decision items |

Only 4 and 5 survive without questioning. The rebuild starts from "attention is short" and "decisions are the real work", not from "a 60 minute meeting".
